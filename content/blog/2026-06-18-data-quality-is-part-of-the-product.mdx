## The invisible dependency

Every score, regime label, and sentiment reading rests on data you usually never see. If that data is stale, incomplete, or silently substituted, the output is confidently wrong — and nothing on the surface tells you.

That is why data quality is not a preprocessing chore at Aivex. It is a product concern with its own outputs.

## Quarantine over silent substitution

The single most important rule: stale data is quarantined, not silently used. Above a configurable freshness threshold, a field is flagged rather than quietly filled in with the last value we happened to have. A visible gap is far safer than an invisible guess.

## Routing, fallback, and provenance

When a provider is unavailable, routing and fallback let the system degrade gracefully — but it records which provider actually served each field. Provenance is not a nicety; it is what lets you trust, or distrust, a reading later.

Values are normalized before anything compares them: units aligned, timestamps to UTC, identifiers reconciled. Comparisons are only meaningful when they are like-for-like.

## Reproducible reports

The output of this work is a data-quality report that is reproducible: given the same inputs, you get the same report. That reproducibility is what makes the rest of the research defensible — a result you cannot reproduce is a story, not a finding.

## Where it connects

This is the job of **Metrics & Validation**: score the reliability of every other lens before anyone trusts it. Data quality is the foundation that scoring stands on, and it is a first-class part of the product — not a step we hide.
